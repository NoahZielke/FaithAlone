The Euthyphro Dilemma - A Non-Dilemma by the Greeks, Who Got (Nearly) Everything Wrong

The Euthyphro Dilemma is the name of an alleged dilemma from the author Plato (4th century BCE), in which a character in his work Euthyphro asks:

Plato - Euthyphro

Is the good loved by the gods because it is good, or is it good because it is loved by the gods?

The question is supposed to present a dilemma for God that goes something like this:

  • Suppose that God always does that which is "good". Does God then do it because there is some rule which He is bowing to, that exists beyond and independent of Him? Or, is "good" defined as "whatever God does", such that it is arbitrary, and God could in theory change whether a thing is good or evil depending on His whim?

However, the whole phrasing of the problem is very strange, and creates confusion by presenting "good" as arbitrary in both branches of the dilemma. In the first branch, it is some arbitrary, eternal "rule" embedded in reality. In the other, it is arbitrary, being a function of God's whim.

It is better to frame the issue as follows:

  • God is omniscient. As such, God is the most intelligent being in existence
  • God, unlike human beings, always operates according to that which makes logical sense. And, since God is omniscient, God operates according to perfect logic
  • That which comports with perfect logic is "good"
  • Therefore, God certainly always does that which is "good", but that is a function of the fact that God is supremely rational. It is not something which lords over Him, nor is it capricious

Rationality, therefore, is the basis for determining that which is "good". Human beings can ascertain that to the degree that they are rational. God, being supremely rational, embodies goodness entirely. There is no real dilemma in any of this.
