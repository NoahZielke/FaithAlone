Evaluating Arguments for the Existence of God

This article will examine the major arguments for God's existence, and classify them based on their relative strength.

The arguments' premises (P) and conclusions (C) will be presented first. Then, they will be evaluated. After evaluation, they will each receive a rating of: Strong, Medium, or Weak.

The Cosmological Argument

One way of presenting the Cosmological Argument is:

The Cosmological Argument is, by itself, not an argument for the existence of God. Technically speaking, it is an argument for a first cause. It needs to be paired with another argument in order to get from "there is a first cause" to "there is an intelligent first cause". An Atheist could agree with all of the premises, but believe in an unintelligent first cause.

However, in my opinion, it firmly establishes that there must be a first cause, and so accomplishes that objective. It is strong for what it actually argues for, but since it does not actually get one to God by itself, it gets a "weak" rating for the purposes of this article.

Rating: Weak
